This bike is what got me into building bikes. This isn’t a built bike, as in it’s basically the exact same as how I got it, but it’s definitely not stock. I still remember riding around in the parking lot of the bike coop where I got it and realizing what I had been missing out on my entire life. 

The frame is a Univega Alpina 5.5 which has these great ovalized tubes and is incredibly light. It’s got a Shimano deore groupset with these silly low profile cantilever brakes that are so much harder to set up properly than the older ones. These will definitely get swapped out for some nice v brakes once I have some more disposable income, but for now I swapped out the front pads for some KoolStop ones and tried to get the yoke as close as I could to the tire which is good enough for now.

The bars are mystery no name cruiser bars (highly recommended tilting swept bars down so that more of your weight is over the front wheel) that are remarkably similar to the Velo Orange tourist bars which I’d love to put on but would just be an aesthetic change. Speaking of aesthetics, I’ve been debating recently whether to go full chrome. I’d need to change the bars, cranks, and brakes. But is that the spirit of the build? It’s been a bike I can use in all situations, especially once I put the rear rack on it. I took it on a 100 mile bikepacking trip, rode it to school all year, and take it for gravel rides whenever the mood finds me. Does it need to be all chrome? Would I care about it more, and therefore ride it less, if it was?

All questions that I don’t have the answer to. I’ve also loved the look of these bikes without the rear rack lately and I’m making myself some frame bags to make up for the lack of storage space if I decide to take it off. But it doubles so well as a rear fender which makes me think about getting fenders. You see where I’m going with this? I think that the best thing to do with this bike for now is just to ride it. Ride it everywhere all the time. Maybe once I move I’ll make it a true rigid mountain bike for day rides and build up a beater single speed for commuting. I’m thinking of a 26” step through frame with swept bars, vees, bmx tires, maybe a 40/12 gearing, and a big front rack. That sounds good. But for now, this is the everyday all the time bike.
